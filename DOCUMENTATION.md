# Tier 238:
1. Ghast Pet / Ghast Pet Spawn Egg (a quarter-sized ghast that follows whoever hatched it and throws
   the boss's own homing fire charges at what threatens them - 12 damage a charge, one every three
   seconds, out to 24 blocks, and no Ghasted at all: a pet that hung a screaming flock on everything

   drops nothing.
   Its egg is a `PetSpawnEggItem`, and that class exists because vanilla's spawn path has nowhere to
   say who a mob belongs to - an ownerless pet hunts players, so a Ghast Pet out of an ordinary egg
   would turn on the person who hatched it)
2. `AbstractFriendlyGhastEntity` (a ghast that is not a monster, and the base both the pet and the
   mount are built on. It exists because no amount of overriding could have answered the problem:
   `Ghast` implements `Enemy`, a marker interface, and an iron golem's targeting goal looks for mobs
   that are `instanceof Enemy` - so a pet or a mount built on `Ghast` is something every golem in
   every village charges at, and something every `Enemy`-shaped rule in every other mod treats as a
   threat. A subclass cannot un-implement an interface, so the only fix is to not be one: this
   extends `FlyingMob`, which is what `Ghast` itself extends and where all of its flight comes from,
   and copies the three small things worth keeping - the moan, the charging flag the renderer draws
   as an open mouth, and a ghast's sound volume. Its sounds go out on the neutral channel rather than
   the hostile one, which is the one audible difference.
   Nothing is lost by it. `GhastModel` is generic over `Entity` rather than over `Ghast`, so
   `FriendlyGhastRenderer` draws both with vanilla's own model and both of vanilla's textures)
3. `PetAi` (the third writing of wolf AI for something that is not a `TamableAnimal`, and the last:
   the follow goal, the owner-combat goal and the enemy predicate are now in one file and the
   Compressed Phantom and Compressed Silverfish read them from there. A pet is three facts - who owns
   it, which of its owner's fights it has already joined, and how to write that down)

# Tier 239:
1. Ghast Mount / Ghast Mount Spawn Egg (a full-sized ghast with no goals whatsoever, which is what
   makes it a mount: left alone it hangs exactly where it was put, and everything it does a rider
   does. Right-click empty-handed to get on. It flies where you look - the forward key is split
   between climb and travel by the pitch of the rider's head, so there is no separate ascend key and
   no need for one - and settles at 0.8 blocks a tick, a little over a sprint. `FlyingMob`'s own
   flight hard-codes an acceleration of 0.02 that no attribute can reach, which over its 0.91 of drag
   is slower than walking, so `travel` is overridden to use the mount's own figure. 60 hp and full
   knockback resistance: being shoved off course by every arrow that hits it is the one thing that
   would make it unflyable. No gravity and no fall damage, so a rider who dismounts three hundred
   blocks up falls on their own account.
   <br>Its back is a platform saddle, and that is the reason to own one that a pet ghast does not
   answer: a nine-by-nine deck, up to four layers and sixty-four blocks in all, that can be built on
   and flown. Right-click it with a full block to lay one and hit a block to take it back - the block
   goes into your inventory rather than onto the ground, the ground on a mount usually being two
   hundred blocks down. Only full cubes, and nothing with an inventory: a cell is one collision cube
   and has nowhere to keep contents. The deck is the entity's own rather than the world's, so no
   chunk is ever written, the whole of it is saved on the mount and comes back as items if the mount
   dies, and the pilot's seat moves from the creature's back to the floor as soon as there is a floor
   to stand on. Three pieces make it work. `MountPlatform` is the grid - blocks rather than block
   states, a hit test that answers both placing and breaking, and a greedy merge that cuts the deck
   into as few boxes as cover it, so a plain floor is one box however wide. `PlatformColliderEntity`
   is one of those boxes: an entity's bounding box is the only surface a mod can put in the air
   without a mixin, which is the same trick that lets you stand on a boat, and it puts itself where
   its mount is every tick on both sides rather than being synced by position. And the mount carries
   whatever is standing on the deck itself, because nothing in vanilla moves an entity that is merely
   standing on another one - done on whichever side owns what is being moved, the server for
   everything and the client for its own player, since a player's position is their own client's to
   decide)
